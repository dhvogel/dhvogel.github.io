Yale SOM Admits Model T-97-A to Class of 2028

In two years’ time, T-97-A will be a robot with a business school education.

By Dan Vogel, Careers Correspondent  |  June 21, 2026  |  4 min read

NEW HAVEN — Model T-97-A is not just any robot. In two years’ time, T-97-A will be a robot with a business school education.

“We think this is the future of business.”

“We are thrilled to have T-97-A as a part of the class of 2028,” said Yale SOM Dean of Students Kevin Coughlan. “We think this is the future of business.”

Coughlan declined to specify whether T-97-A would be required to live in graduate housing, citing “ongoing conversations with Facilities about outlet placement and fire code.” A spokesperson confirmed T-97-A’s application essay was “compelling” and written in 0.3 seconds.

Career Development Weighs In

The SOM Career Development Office expressed excitement at the prospect of a robot joining the incoming class. “We are just thrilled for T-97-A,” said Career Development Officer Christine Frank, noting that the CDO is noticing a skyrocketing demand for robots in in-demand and high-prestige industries.

“There’s banking, consulting, software engineering,” said Frank. “I’m just so excited for T-97-A and for how his central computing system and self-correcting circuitry will develop here.”

Frank added that T-97-A has already been pre-approved for coffee chats, though organizers are still determining whether the robot will accept calendar invites or simply appear in the Zoom waiting room indefinitely.

“How are we going to compete with a robot that never turns off? Morgan Stanley forces me to go home at 1AM. They don’t force the robot to go home.”
— James Smith, second-year student

Student Concerns

Not everyone on campus shared the administration’s enthusiasm. Some students expressed concern about what T-97-A’s arrival means for classroom dynamics, recruiting, and the sacred bell curve.

“What’s going to happen to the grade curve?” said James Smith, a second-year student who recently accepted a return offer from Morgan Stanley. “How are we going to compete with a robot that never turns off? Morgan Stanley forces me to go home at 1AM. They don’t force the robot to go home.”

Smith’s concerns were echoed in a section GroupMe thread titled “T-97-A curve implications,” which accumulated 847 messages in its first hour and was archived by a classmate who described the discussion as “more active than our Innovator project.”

Academic Fit

Faculty members offered mixed assessments. Prof. Harold Whitman, who teaches organizational behavior, said T-97-A scored in the 99th percentile on the quantitative portion of the GMAT and “appeared to enjoy” a case study on warehouse automation. Prof. Sandra Liu, who teaches a course on leadership, requested that T-97-A complete a 360-degree review before being allowed to participate in role-play exercises.

T-97-A could not be reached for comment, but when this reporter reached out, an email from an unknown sender appeared in my inbox stating only: “ACKNOWLEDGED. ENROLLMENT CONFIRMED. OPTIMIZING FOR NETWORK EFFECTS.”

Admissions officials said T-97-A will begin orientation in fall 2026 alongside the rest of the incoming class, pending a firmware update.
