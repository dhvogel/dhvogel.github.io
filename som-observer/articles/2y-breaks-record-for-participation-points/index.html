2Y Breaks Record For Participation Points

Chad Ryerson averaged 847 hand-raises per semester on his way to a record 125 participation points, shattering the previous benchmark set during the Zoom era when cameras could remain off.

By Dan Vogel, Campus Correspondent  |  June 21, 2026  |  5 min read

NEW HAVEN — There’s a new sheriff in town. Chad Ryerson, a second-year student from Pelican Rapids, Minnesota, has broken the Yale School of Management record for most participation points earned across a full MBA, capping his run with a perfect five out of five in every Spring 2 course.

Ryerson will graduate with a grand total of 125 participation points over his two years at SOM, edging out prior record holder Tyler Stevenson, who held the benchmark at 124. Stevenson’s bid fell short after he inexplicably earned only a four out of five in Spring 2 Innovator, despite being widely regarded as one of the top contributors in the class. The incident has since entered campus lore as “The Stevenson Moment.”

“You know, Chad, it might just be possible. And it was.”

When asked how he achieved the feat, Ryerson was characteristically modest. “You know, I never intended to come here and break the record,” he said. “Of course, I knew about the Stevenson Moment. We all knew about the Stevenson Moment. But at the end of first year I looked at my participation point total and I said to myself, ‘you know, Chad, it might just be possible.’ And it was.”

The Stevenson Moment

Until this spring, Stevenson’s 124-point total had stood as the gold standard of SOM classroom engagement—a number spoken of in section lounges with the reverence usually reserved for perfect GMAT scores and pre-recruiting timelines that begin before orientation ends.

Stevenson had maintained a flawless five-out-of-five streak through nearly every course in the curriculum. Then came Innovator. Classmates say he contributed consistently, raised his hand with purpose, and once connected a case on supply chain resilience to an anecdote from his time at a growth-stage fintech. He received a four.

“It didn’t make sense then and it doesn’t make sense now. Tyler was one of the best contributors in that room.”
— Unnamed sectionmate, who spoke on condition of anonymity because “participation is subjective until it isn’t”

Theories abound. Some believe the professor was sending a message about quality over quantity. Others suspect a clerical error that was never corrected. A third camp maintains that Stevenson simply peaked too early, like a company that hits profitability in Year One and spends Year Two explaining the margin compression.

Stevenson, reached for comment, declined to relitigate the grade. “I’m proud of what I built here,” he said. “Chad earned it. I just wish Innovator had earned me.”

The Ryerson Run

Ryerson’s path to 125 was less dramatic on the surface and more methodical underneath. Peers describe him as the rare student who read the case before the case prep session, spoke when he had something to add, and resisted the urge to open every comment with “building on what she said.”

His Spring 2 slate included no weak links. Accounting, State and Society, a second-year elective on competitive strategy, and Innovator—the same course that ended Stevenson’s streak—all returned fives. Faculty in Innovator confirmed Ryerson’s participation score without elaborating on Stevenson’s, citing “academic discretion and an abundance of caution.”

Administrators noted that 125 points represents the theoretical maximum under the current rubric, assuming twenty-five graded courses at five points each. “We don’t hand these out,” said one registrar official. “But Chad didn’t leave any on the table.”

Campus Reaction

News of the record spread quickly through Evans Hall. First-year students reportedly pulled up their own running totals and performed back-of-the-envelope math. Several concluded that the Stevenson Moment had made the record feel both attainable and haunted—like buying a house in a neighborhood where one property sold for slightly under ask three years ago and everyone still talks about it.

Ryerson said he plans to list the achievement on his LinkedIn under “Honors & Awards,” directly above his entry for the Pelican Rapids High School Future Business Leaders of America chapter. He has not yet decided whether to add “125/125” to his email signature.

As for Stevenson, classmates say he has been gracious in defeat, though one reported seeing him linger outside an Innovator classroom last week, staring at the door. “He wasn’t enrolled,” the classmate said. “I think he was just processing.”

Ryerson, for his part, offered a parting thought as Commencement approached. “Tyler set the bar,” he said. “The Stevenson Moment just showed the rest of us where the bar could wobble.”

About the author: Dan Vogel covers campus life and participation point historiography for SOM Observer. He was in the room for the Stevenson Moment and has not recovered.
