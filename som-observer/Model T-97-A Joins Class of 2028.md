Model T-97-A is not just any robot. In two years time, T-97-A will be a robot with a business school education. 

“We are thrilled to have T-97-A as a part of the class of 2028,” said Yale SOM Dean of Students Kevin Coughlan. “We think this is the future of business.”

The SOM Career Development Office expressed excitement at the prospect of a robot joining the incoming class. “We are just thrilled for T-97-A,” said Career Development Officer Christine Frank, noting that the CDO is noticing a skyrocketing demand for robots in in-demand and high-prestige industries. “There’s banking, consulting, software engineering,” said Frank. “I’m just so excited for T-97-A and for how his central computing system and self-correcting circuitry will develop here.”

Some students expressed concern. “What’s going to happen to the grade curve?” said James Smith. “How are we going to compete with a robot that never turns off? Morgan Stanley forces me to go home at 1AM. They don’t force the robot to go home.”
